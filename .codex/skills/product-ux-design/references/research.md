# Research and usability
Read when user needs are uncertain or research/usability testing is requested. Pick the question first; choose interviews, observation, task-based tests or other methods to answer it. Obtain appropriate consent, minimize personal data and keep recruitment/contact within owner authorization. Write realistic non-leading tasks; record sampling limits and observations separately from interpretations. Do not fabricate participants, findings, statistics or accessibility validation. Define task completion, errors, recovery and unresolved assumptions. Specify pending/error/retry states: guard unsafe repeat activation immediately, preserve input on failure, and choose spinner delay for observed latency. Choose validation timing from task and user evidence; avoid interrupting unfinished input. Link submission error summaries to fields where useful; use plain recovery copy and specific action labels. Existing brief and credible evidence may suffice for a small flow change; do not force research for every UI edit. Hand off primary tasks, information hierarchy, meaningful states and content.
